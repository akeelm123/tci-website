Case study 3

Agile transformation without leadership change

Teams trained. Ceremonies introduced. Delivery cadence improved. But decision-making remained slow, funding stayed annual, and priorities changed weekly.

Executive concern

Why isn't team agility translating into enterprise agility?

Funding was still annual and project-based. Senior leaders continued to request detailed upfront plans. Teams reported that priorities changed frequently. The transformation leader wanted to understand the gap.

Executive interpretation

The organisation has improved delivery mechanics but not the leadership system around delivery.

Agile practices are visible, but transformation confidence remains low because executive decision-making, funding, prioritisation and value management still operate in traditional ways. The organisation may be doing agile without becoming more adaptive.

Recommended actions

30

Stabilise

Reconfirm the business outcomes the agile transformation is meant to achieve. Identify leadership behaviours slowing decision-making. Review whether teams have stable priorities and empowered product ownership.

90

Strengthen

Shift governance from project status to product value. Align funding to outcomes rather than fixed project scope. Train senior leaders on their role in enterprise agility. Create metrics around decision speed and team autonomy.

180

Sustain

Embed product-based governance. Track measurable improvements in time-to-market and business responsiveness. Review whether leadership behaviours are reinforcing or undermining agility.

"Agile transformation does not fail because teams cannot adopt new practices. It fails when leadership continues to operate using old assumptions."
