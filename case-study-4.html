Case study 4

Platform engineering without internal customer adoption

The platform team built reusable capabilities, automated pipelines and internal tools. Technical maturity was improving. But adoption across application teams remained uneven.

Executive concern

Enterprise leverage or another central function?

Several teams continued building their own tooling. Some developers saw the platform as restrictive. Business stakeholders did not yet see measurable delivery improvement.

Executive interpretation

The platform is technically capable but has not yet become an enterprise product.

Confidence is limited because adoption, user experience, value measurement and behavioural change are weak. The platform team is building capability, but internal customers have not fully changed how they deliver.

Recommended actions

30

Stabilise

Segment internal customers and understand adoption barriers. Define platform product ownership. Identify duplicated tooling and reasons for non-adoption. Establish developer experience feedback loops.

90

Strengthen

Publish platform service catalogue and adoption roadmap. Introduce internal customer success metrics. Measure onboarding time, deployment frequency, quality and developer satisfaction.

180

Sustain

Shift platform success metrics from availability to enterprise leverage. Track reuse, adoption, speed, resilience and cost avoidance. Embed platform governance into technology strategy and portfolio planning.

"A platform only creates value when people choose to use it. Adoption is not a communication problem. It is a product, culture and value problem."
