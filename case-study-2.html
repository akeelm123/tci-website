Case study 2

Enterprise data platform with low data confidence

A financial services organisation built an enterprise data platform. Strong sponsorship, strategic importance. But business teams challenged the accuracy and ownership of critical data.

Executive concern

Better decisions or another centralised capability?

The Board wanted to know whether the platform would improve decision-making or simply create another technology layer. The CDO and Chief Platform Owner were concerned that adoption would stall unless data trust improved.

Executive interpretation

Strategic intent is strong, but confidence is limited by weak trust in data.

The programme is at risk of becoming a platform build rather than a data value transformation. Without clear data ownership, quality standards, metadata, lineage and business accountability, the organisation may struggle to realise value from analytics and AI.

Recommended actions

30

Stabilise

Identify the most critical data domains and elements. Assign accountable business data owners. Prioritise quality issues affecting business decisions. Define minimum evidence for trusted data use.

90

Strengthen

Implement data ownership and governance routines. Establish metadata and lineage standards. Link platform delivery to specific business use cases. Create data product adoption and trust metrics.

180

Sustain

Embed data confidence reporting into executive governance. Measure improvements in decision quality, regulatory confidence and analytics adoption. Expand from platform delivery to data product value realisation.

"A data platform does not create value because data has been centralised. It creates value when data becomes trusted, governed, usable and connected to business decisions."
